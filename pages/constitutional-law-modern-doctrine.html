About This eBook

The United States Constitution is a living document — shaped not just by its framers, but by the countless Supreme Court decisions that have interpreted it across two centuries. In today's polarized legal landscape, understanding modern constitutional doctrine is not optional for any serious practitioner or judicial professional; it is essential.

Constitutional Law: Modern Doctrine was written for practicing attorneys, law clerks, judges, and advanced law students who need a precise, current command of constitutional jurisprudence — not an academic survey, but a working reference grounded in the Supreme Court's most significant recent decisions and doctrinal shifts.

From the Court's evolving First Amendment framework to the seismic shifts in substantive due process, equal protection doctrine, and the newly reinvigorated non-delegation principle, this guide traces how modern doctrine developed and how skilled advocates deploy it in litigation today.
